Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

Lupus is a chronic autoimmune disease that can affect the joints, skin, kidneys, blood cells, heart, lungs, nervous system, and other parts of the body. Symptoms vary widely and may come and go.

What is lupus?

In systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), the immune system attacks the body’s own tissues, causing inflammation. People may experience periods of increased disease activity, called flares, and periods when symptoms improve.

Possible symptoms

  • Painful or swollen joints and morning stiffness
  • Fatigue or unexplained fever
  • Rashes, including a rash across the cheeks and nose
  • Sensitivity to sunlight, hair loss, or sores in the mouth or nose
  • Color changes in the fingers or toes with cold or stress
  • Chest pain with deep breathing, leg swelling, or other organ-related symptoms

How lupus is evaluated

No single test diagnoses lupus. A rheumatology evaluation may include the pattern and history of symptoms, a physical examination, blood counts, kidney function tests, urine testing, ANA and more specific antibody tests. A positive ANA alone does not establish a lupus diagnosis.

Treatment and monitoring

Treatment depends on which parts of the body are affected and how active the disease is. Options may include antimalarial medicines, anti-inflammatory medicines, corticosteroids, immunosuppressive therapies, or biologic medicines. Regular follow-up helps monitor disease activity, organ health, medication effectiveness, and potential side effects.

Seek urgent care: New chest pain, difficulty breathing, confusion, seizure, severe headache, or rapidly increasing swelling requires prompt medical attention.
