Gout

Gout is an inflammatory arthritis caused by urate crystals that form in and around joints. It often produces sudden episodes of intense pain, swelling, redness, and warmth.

What causes gout?

Gout develops when urate accumulates over time and forms needle-shaped crystals. A high blood urate level alone does not necessarily mean that a person has gout, and several other conditions can resemble a gout flare.

Common symptoms

  • Sudden, severe pain in one or more joints
  • Swelling, warmth, redness, and marked tenderness
  • Flares that often begin in the big toe or another lower-limb joint
  • Symptom-free periods between attacks
  • Firm urate deposits called tophi in longstanding disease

How gout is diagnosed

Evaluation may include the history and pattern of attacks, a joint examination, blood urate testing, and imaging. When needed, fluid can be removed from a swollen joint and examined for urate crystals. This can also help distinguish gout from infection.

Treatment and prevention

A gout flare may be treated with an anti-inflammatory medicine, colchicine, or a corticosteroid, depending on the individual. For patients who need long-term urate lowering, treatment may reduce future flares and help prevent tophi and joint damage. Healthy weight, limiting alcohol and sugar-sweetened drinks, and addressing related kidney or cardiovascular risks may support medical treatment.

Seek same-day evaluation: A first episode of a severely painful, hot, swollen joint—particularly with fever or illness—could be a joint infection and should not be assumed to be gout.
